# Hackathon Rules & Terms (Qollab x IonQ)

Source: official Qollab hackathon rules, copied for reference. Check the Qollab website for the authoritative version.

## Rules & terms

Qollab.xyz and IonQ will run a distributed global hackathon on October 9th to 11th, 2026. During the hackathon, participants will build quantum applications that are published on Qollab on an MIT license, enabling others to find them, fork them and build on them.

All submitted projects will be evaluated by judging panels based on a common rubric focused on Novelty & Creativity, Technical Execution, Impact Potential and Documentation Quality. The projects that receive the highest evaluations based on the rubric will be eligible for cash and other awards as stipulated in the Judging section of this website.

## Eligibility & team structure

### Teams and tracks

- Team size: participants can compete solo or in teams of up to 4 members.
- Teams register in one of two tracks, student or professional:
  - To register as a student team, every member must be enrolled in an undergraduate or graduate program at the university identified in our registration form as the team's "university hackathon node".
  - All other teams register as professionals, including teams that combine students and professionals. During registration, professional teams also associate themselves with a "university hackathon node". We recommend they select either one that is close to them geographically or one by which they are affiliated (e.g. being alumni).
- Everyone on a team competes at the same node. We cannot accommodate mixed node teams.
- Both tracks are scored on the same rubric and compete for the same awards. Tracks let university nodes run student-only events and add student-specific awards at their discretion.
- Participants may submit multiple projects. Awards are per project, not per person or team.
- Participants do not need to have any prior quantum computing experience.
- Participants need to be at least 18 years old.

### Eligible countries

Participants can be based in any of the following countries: Argentina, Australia, Austria, Barbados, Belgium, Brazil, Bulgaria, Canada, Chile, Colombia, Cyprus, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, India, Indonesia, Ireland, Israel, Italy, Japan, Latvia, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malaysia, Malta, Mexico, Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Singapore, Slovakia, South Korea, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, the United Kingdom, and the United States.

## Submission requirements

Every project must

- Use the Qiskit framework
- Follow industry best practices on UI accessibility
- Run on Qollab's free simulators, including IonQ's quantum simulator. Judges run every project on a free local simulator, and results from IonQ's simulators or hardware neither add nor subtract points.
- Be published and runnable on Qollab.xyz on an MIT license before the submission deadline
- Use an MIT license for any outside repo the project relies on, such as a frontend or backend hosted elsewhere
- Have a project page on Qollab.xyz covering what the submission does, what problem it explores, how to run it, and other information to enable others to understand, replicate, or extend it
- Be built during the hackathon window. Existing code, templates and forks are fine as a starting point when disclosed, as Originality below explains; judging is based on what the team adds during the window.
- Include a demo video of 2 to 3 minutes, at a public link that opens without signing in

## Themes

Projects should leverage quantum computing's unique attributes and cover one or more of the following themes:

**Quantum Impact** — solve real-world problems with quantum + classical computing. Take on a real challenge in logistics, manufacturing, space, finance, energy, or another industry using a hybrid quantum-classical approach. Choose a relevant public dataset, build a strong classical baseline, then identify where a quantum subroutine can make a difference. Show us the impact — from better solution quality to improved efficiency, accuracy, or other measurable gains.

**Quantum Playground** — use quantum computing to build something playful, artistic, or unexpected. Turn quantum mechanics into a creative medium. Build a game, compose music, generate art, design an interactive experience, or invent something we haven't imagined yet. The challenge: make quantum science an essential part of the experience, using concepts such as superposition, interference, entanglement, or measurement in a way that goes beyond simply generating randomness.

**Quantum Unlocked** — make the hardest ideas in quantum computing click. Quantum computing is full of fascinating ideas that can be difficult to visualize and understand. Pick a timely topic, such as quantum error correction, fault tolerance, quantum networking, or algorithms, and find a creative way to teach it. Build an interactive demo, game, animation, visualization, or GUI that turns a complex concept into something intuitive, engaging, and memorable.

Projects that don't fall under one of these themes are still welcome and eligible for placement awards (1st/2nd/3rd and node-level prizes), but will not be eligible for Theme specialty awards.

## Beyond the tutorial bar

Each submission must go beyond a standard reference implementation or tutorial-level exercise and demonstrate at least one of the following:

- A novel application or problem context (e.g., applying an existing technique to a specific domain)
- A technical extension (e.g., noise/error mitigation, parameter optimization, comparative benchmarking)
- An original analytical or educational contribution (e.g., a genuinely new way of visualizing or explaining a quantum phenomenon)

The judging panel will assess these traits as part of the Novelty & Creativity and Technical Execution rubric criteria. Panels may score a submission at 0 on these criteria, independent of other scores, if it fails to meet this bar.

## Award structure

The judging panel assigned to each university node will score all relevant submissions using a global rubric described in the judging section of this website.

### Local judging

- If there are fewer than 10 projects submitted, the judging panel will evaluate all node entries and will advance the top 3 to the global judging process.
- If there are 10 or more submitted projects, the judging panel will provide teams with the following awards — and all three advance to the global judging process:

| Place | Cash | Compute |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | $500 | $5,000 |
| 2nd | $300 | $3,000 |
| 3rd | $200 | $2,000 |

Regardless of the number of projects submitted, the judging panel for each node will also have the discretion to advance up to one project for each of the specialized global awards: Most Novel / Creative, Best of Theme, Most Impact, Best Documentation. A project can advance to the global judging phase both for overall placement (top 3 spots) and as a candidate for a specialized award.

### Global judging

The global judging panel will score all submissions advanced from university node judging panels using the same rubric and will provide teams with the following awards:

| Place | Cash | Compute |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | $10,000 | $20,000 |
| 2nd | $5,000 | $15,000 |
| 3rd | $2,500 | $10,000 |
| Specialty | $500 | $5,000 |

Specialty awards: Most Novel / Creative, Best of each of the three themes, Most Impact, Best Documentation. A project can win both a placement prize and a specialty award.

Select teams may receive additional funds, computing resources and other awards at the discretion of Qollab, IonQ and/or any other participating sponsor. University nodes can and are encouraged to add supplemental prizes at their own discretion to reward student-specific effort.

## Originality & code of conduct

Projects must be substantially new work built during the hackathon window. Using AI, open-source libraries, prior tutorials, or Qollab-hosted templates as a starting point is fine and encouraged. The differentiator is what teams build on top of them, not the whole submission.

Plagiarism, misrepresented authorship, or submitting someone else's unmodified work will result in disqualification. This includes but is not limited to:

- Submitting a pre-existing project (in whole or substantial part) as new work
- Copying code, circuit designs, or written explanations from external sources (papers, repos, tutorials, other participants) without disclosure and attribution
- Using a Qollab-hosted template or open-source library as a starting point without disclosure
- Two or more teams submitting substantially identical projects without independent development

Standard hackathon code of conduct applies at every node: harassment-free environment, respectful collaboration, no discrimination. Each node's local organizers are responsible for enforcement on-site; Qollab handles platform-level and cross-node conduct issues.

## Disputes & appeals

### Appeals

All scoring and award determinations are final and not subject to appeal.

The sole exception is disqualification: a participant or team disqualified under the Originality & Code of Conduct section may submit a written appeal within 24 hours of notification, addressed to hello@qollab.xyz. Appeals are limited to disputing the factual basis for disqualification (e.g., a claim that the work was in fact original, or that an eligibility determination was made in error). A designated reviewer, separate from the original judging panel, will issue a final decision within 5 business days. That decision is final and not subject to further appeal.

### No reallocation of disqualified awards

If a team or project is disqualified, whether before or after an award is announced, that award or advancement slot is withdrawn and is not reassigned to the next-highest-scoring team. This applies to both:

- Placement and prizes: a disqualified team's cash, compute, or specialty award is withdrawn and not given to the runner-up.
- Advancement: if a disqualified project would have advanced from local to global judging (or from global judging to a final award tier), that slot is withdrawn and not backfilled by the next-ranked project. The relevant node or round simply advances one fewer project.

### Return of distributed awards

If disqualification is determined after an award has already been paid out or compute credits issued, the participant or team agrees to return the full cash award and forfeit any unused compute credits within 30 days of notification. Qollab reserves the right to pursue recovery of distributed funds and to bar the individuals involved from future Qollab hackathons.

## Platform availability & force majeure

Qollab and IonQ will make reasonable efforts to ensure the Qollab platform and IonQ simulator are available throughout the build window. However, neither Qollab, IonQ, nor participating sponsors are responsible for outages, degraded performance, or other technical disruptions to the platform or simulator, including those caused by circumstances beyond their reasonable control (force majeure), such as third-party infrastructure failures, cyberattacks, or acts of nature.

### Outages over 12 hours during the build window

- Qollab will communicate the outage and expected resolution time via Slack as soon as reasonably possible.
- At Qollab's discretion, the build window and/or submission deadline may be extended by a period reasonably proportional to the outage duration, applied uniformly across all nodes.
- If an extension is not feasible (e.g., an outage occurs very close to the original deadline), Qollab may instead accept late submissions with a documented outage-impact statement, at the judging panel's discretion, without penalty to the affected team's score.
- Qollab is not obligated to extend the deadline for outages under the stated threshold, brief or intermittent issues, or problems isolated to a single participant's local setup (e.g., their own internet connection).

## Participant obligations

Participants agree to

- Release code and documentation under an MIT license
- Make their projects available on Qollab for 12 months
- Include the following attribution on their project: "This effort is supported by Qollab & IonQ"
- Provide a payment method in the chance they win a monetary award

Award winners additionally agree to

- Allow Qollab and IonQ to feature their work and profile, and to name the university hackathon node selected for their entry, in press releases and other marketing activities.
- Agree they are solely responsible for tax reporting
- Post at least once on their social channels about their participation in the hackathon
