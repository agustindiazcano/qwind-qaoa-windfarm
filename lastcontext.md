Nothing was tuned after seeing the results; the lam sweep (1, 3, 10 x max|h|) and the ramp total time (0.75 p, placeholder) were fixed beforehand.
  - Penalty + X mixer (the old QAOA) does NOT reliably beat uniform sampling over feasible states: feasible-only approximation ratio beats the uniform baseline in 29-86% of instances depending on lam and p, mean ratio 0.52-0.64 vs uniform 0.529, and P(optimal) is below the uniform-over-feasible value in most settings.   - XY mixer + Dicke start (lam = 0 in the circuit): P(feasible) = 1 by construction, feasible-only ratio 0.67-0.72 vs uniform 0.529 and it beats the uniform baseline in 100% of instances at p = 1, 2, 3. P(optimal) 0.082 / 0.110 / 0.150 (p = 1, 2, 3) vs uniform 0.026 (about 3-6x). The linear-ramp initialization helps the ratio at p = 2, 3 (0.703, 0.720) but lowers P(optimal) (0.067, 0.118), and it does nothing for the penalty variant.
  - Caveat: at these sizes a random feasible sample of 1000 subsets already hits the optimum with probability ~0.999, and so does sampling the xy+dicke QAOA at p = 3 (~1.0), so a "best of 1000 shots" row cannot separate them. The advantage is in the distribution (P(optimal), ratio), not in the best sample. Instances are small, simulator only, mean over only 7 instances, so no claim beyond this benchmark.
  - Dicke runs optimize on the statevector of the Dicke state directly (StatePreparation is ~10x slower per evaluation); the full circuit with StatePreparation is used for sampling and is checked against the Estimator in tests.
